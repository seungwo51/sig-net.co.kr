Summary

  • A Korea KTV bill is the drink set plus TC for each guest plus the room fee. At Signet in Gangnam that is ₩460,000 for two.
  • Guests must be 19 or older and show a passport. A photo of the passport is not accepted.
  • Book by message from abroad and ask for the total in writing before you arrive.

KTV works differently in every country. In Korea you do not pay per hour or per song. You pay for a set, a charge per guest and the room. First-time visitors who know this in advance are not surprised by the bill.

How KTV works in Korea

Five words cover almost every price quote you will get.

Words on a Korea KTV bill

WordMeaning
Judae (주대)The basic drink set, usually whisky with mixers. Charged once per room.
TCTable charge. Charged for each guest. ₩130,000 at Signet.
Room fee (룸비)Charged once per room. ₩50,000.
Extension (연장)Staying past the basic time. TC becomes ₩140,000 per guest.
Part 1 / Part 2 (1부·2부)Evening and late-night time bands. Some venues lower the drink set price late at night or before 9 pm.

Rules for foreign visitors

  • Be 19 or older. This is the legal age for these venues in Korea.
  • Bring your passport itself, not a photo.
  • Get the total for your group in writing before you go.
  • If the number of guests changes, tell us before you leave your hotel.

Payment methods differ by venue. Ask when you book if you plan to pay by foreign card.

FAQ

Q. Is KTV in Korea charged by the hour?

No. You pay for a drink set, TC for each guest and a room fee. Extra time is charged as an extension.

Q. What is TC in a Korean KTV?

Table charge, a fee for each guest. It is ₩130,000 at Signet and ₩140,000 if you extend.

Q. Can foreigners enter a KTV in Korea?

Yes, with a passport and if you are 19 or older.

Q. How do I avoid a surprise bill?

Ask for the total for your number of guests and arrival time, and keep the message. Check it when you pay.
